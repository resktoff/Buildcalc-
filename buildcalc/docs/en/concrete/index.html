How concrete is calculated

The volume of a rectangular slab or footing is the product of its three dimensions: V = length × width × thickness. For a round column we use the cylinder volume: V = π × r² × h, where r is half the diameter.

All measurements are first converted to metres (1 ft = 0.3048 m; 1 in = 0.0254 m), so the internal volume is always in m³. It is then displayed in cubic yards or m³ (1 yd³ = 0.7646 m³).

Waste is added as a percentage of the net volume. 5–10% covers concrete left in the mixer, uneven sub-grade and formwork deflection.

For bags we use the industry rule of thumb: an 80 lb bag yields ≈ 0.60 ft³ (≈ 45 bags per cubic yard). The same yield per kilogram is applied to 25, 40 and 50 kg bags (≈ 2136 kg of dry mix per m³). Always check the yield stated by the manufacturer.

Frequently asked questions

How many bags of concrete do I need for 1 cubic yard?

About 45 bags of 80 lb or 60 bags of 60 lb per cubic yard. In metric, 1 m³ takes roughly 43 bags of 50 kg, 54 of 40 kg or 86 of 25 kg.

What waste percentage should I use?

5% is usually enough for slabs on a well-levelled base; use 10% for hand-dug footings or small pours.

Is thickness entered in inches or feet?

In imperial the thickness is entered in inches (e.g. 4 in) and in metric in centimetres; the calculator converts automatically.

Can I use this to order ready-mix from a truck?

Yes. The total volume with waste is the amount to order; plants usually sell in increments of 0.25 yd³ or 0.5 m³.
