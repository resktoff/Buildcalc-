What this concrete calculator is for and how to use it

Whether you need a concrete slab calculator for a patio or driveway, or a concrete yard calculator to order ready-mix delivery, this tool computes exact volume in cubic yards (yd³), cubic feet (ft³), and cubic meters (m³) before you buy.

How to use it step by step

  1. Choose your unit system: Imperial (feet and inches, outputting yd³ and ft³) or Metric (meters and centimeters, outputting m³).
  2. Select the element shape: Rectangular slab or footing, or Cylindrical column or pier.
  3. Enter the dimensions (length, width, and thickness for slabs; diameter and height for columns) plus the quantity of identical elements.
  4. Set a waste allowance percentage (5%–10% is standard for formed slabs; 10%–15% for uneven subgrades or footings).
  5. Optionally choose a premixed bag size (40 lb, 60 lb, 80 lb, 25 kg, or 50 kg) to see the rounded-up bag count.

Formula, units, and waste calculation

  • Slab or footing: V = Length × Width × Thickness × Quantity
  • Cylindrical column: V = π × (Diameter / 2)² × Height × Quantity
  • Total volume: V_total = V × (1 + Waste / 100)
  • Bags: Count = ceil(V_total / Bag_yield)

Units and conversions: in Imperial mode, thickness is entered in inches and divided by 12 to convert to feet; cubic feet are divided by 27 to get cubic yards (1 yd³ = 27 ft³). In Metric mode, thickness or diameter in centimeters is divided by 100 to convert to meters.

For cylindrical columns or piers, the circular cross-section area π × r² is multiplied by height and quantity.

Material waste: adding 5%–10% waste compensates for uneven subgrades, formwork deflection, compaction, and concrete left in wheelbarrows or pump lines.

Premixed bags divide total volume by standard manufacturer yield (for example, ~0.60 ft³ per 80 lb bag, ~0.45 ft³ per 60 lb bag, or ~0.022 m³ per 50 kg bag), rounded up to whole bags.

How to interpret the results

  • Net volume: exact geometric volume without any loss allowance.
  • Total volume (with waste): the volume to order from a ready-mix supplier or use for bag calculations.
  • Equivalent: shows the same volume in cubic feet or cubic yards for easy comparison.
  • Bags needed: whole number of premixed bags rounded up to cover the total volume.

Practical examples

10 × 10 ft slab at 4 in thick

A standard 10 ft by 10 ft patio slab, 4 inches (0.333 ft) thick, with 10% waste.

10 × 10 × (4/12) = 33.33 ft³ (1.235 yd³) → × 1.10 = 1.358 yd³

Result: 1.358 yd³ total (or 62 bags of 80 lb).

5 × 4 m slab at 10 cm thick

A 5 m × 4 m slab, 10 cm (0.10 m) thick, with 10% waste.

5 × 4 × 0.10 = 2.00 m³ net → 2.00 × 1.10 = 2.20 m³ total

Result: 2.20 m³ of concrete (or 100 bags of 50 kg).

Frequently asked questions

How much concrete do I need for a 10 m² slab?

At 10 cm (0.10 m) thickness, a 10 m² slab needs 1.00 m³ net (1.10 m³ with 10% waste). At 12 cm thickness, it needs 1.20 m³ net (1.32 m³ with 10% waste).

How many 80 lb bags are in a cubic yard of concrete?

One 80 lb premixed bag yields about 0.60 ft³, so 1 cubic yard (27 ft³) requires approximately 45 bags of 80 lb.

What waste percentage should I add?

For formed slabs on a level base, 5%–10% is usually enough. For rough ground excavations or irregular footings, use 10%–15%.

Does this work for mortar or pure cement bags?

Volume (yd³ / m³) applies to any pour, but the bag count assumes premixed concrete bags (with aggregate included), not pure Portland cement.

Do I need to convert inches to feet manually?

No. In imperial mode, thickness is entered directly in inches (in) and converted to feet automatically.
