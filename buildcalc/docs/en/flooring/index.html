How flooring is calculated

The area of a rectangular room is length × width. For L-shaped or irregular rooms, split them into rectangles, add the areas and use the “Area already known” mode.

Waste covers cuts at edges, corners and around obstacles. We recommend 10% for straight layouts, 15% for diagonal or patterned layouts (herringbone, modular) and 5% for large simple rooms.

The number of pieces is the area to buy divided by the area of one piece (length × width), rounded up. Inch or centimetre sizes are converted automatically.

Boxes are the pieces divided by the box contents, or the area to buy divided by the ft² (m²) one box covers, always rounded up. Buy one spare box from the same batch for future repairs.

Frequently asked questions

How many 12 × 24 in tiles do I need for 120 ft²?

Each tile covers 2 ft². With 10% waste the area to buy is 132 ft² → 66 tiles; with 8 tiles per box, 9 boxes.

How much waste should I allow for laminate or vinyl plank?

7–10% for straight layouts. If the room has many corners or you lay diagonally, go up to 15%.

Should I subtract furniture or kitchen cabinets?

Not movable furniture. You can subtract kitchen islands or permanent built-ins using the “Area already known” mode.

Does the calculator account for grout joints?

No: the grout gap (1/16–3/16 in) adds a small margin in your favour that is already covered by the waste percentage.
