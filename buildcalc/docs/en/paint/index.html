What this paint coverage calculator is for and how to use it

Whether you need a wall paint calculator for a single room, a ceiling paint calculator, or a full room paint calculator, this tool estimates exact gallons or liters before you buy.

How to use it step by step

  1. Choose between entering room dimensions (length, width, wall height) or total surface area directly.
  2. Check 'Include ceiling' if you are painting the ceiling with the same paint, and enter the number of doors and windows to subtract.
  3. Set the number of coats (2 coats is standard) and the coverage per coat from your paint can (default: 350 ft²/gal in Imperial or 10 m²/L in Metric).
  4. Add a waste allowance (5%–10% for roller absorption and touch-ups).
  5. Select a container size (1 qt, 1 gal, 5 gal, 1 L, 4 L, or 20 L) to see the rounded-up can count.

Formula and calculation criteria

  • Walls = 2 × (Length + Width) × Height
  • Net area = Walls + Ceiling − Openings
  • Paint = (Net area × Coats / Coverage) × (1 + Waste / 100)
  • Containers = ceil(Paint / Container_size)

Room perimeter is multiplied by wall height to get gross wall area. If ceiling is checked, Length × Width is added.

Standard opening deductions (20 ft² / 1.85 m² per door and 15 ft² / 1.40 m² per window) are subtracted.

Net area is multiplied by the number of coats and divided by single-coat coverage.

Waste allowance is applied and the total is rounded up to whole cans or pails.

How to interpret the results

  • Net area (per coat): actual square footage or square meters covered in one pass.
  • Total area to cover: net area multiplied by the number of coats.
  • Paint needed: total gallons or liters including waste allowance.
  • Containers needed: whole number of cans or pails rounded up.

Practical examples

4 × 3 m bedroom at 2.5 m height (2 coats)

Walls only, 1 door and 1 window, 10 m²/L coverage and 0% waste.

Walls = 35 m² − 3.25 m² openings = 31.75 m² × 2 coats = 63.5 m² ÷ 10 m²/L

Result: 6.35 L of paint (2 cans of 4 L).

400 ft² wall area at 350 ft²/gal (1 coat)

Direct area mode, 1 coat, no openings, 0% waste.

400 ft² ÷ 350 ft²/gal = 1.143 gal

Result: 1.143 gal of paint (2 cans of 1 gal).

Frequently asked questions

How much area does 1 gallon of paint cover?

Standard interior latex paint typically covers 350 to 400 ft² per gallon per coat on smooth, primed drywall. Textured or porous surfaces may drop to 250–300 ft²/gal.

How many coats of paint do I need?

Two coats are standard for even color. Going from a dark color to a light one, or painting bare drywall without primer, may require 3 coats.

Should I include the ceiling in the same calculation?

Only if you are using the exact same paint for both walls and ceiling. If the ceiling uses flat white ceiling paint, calculate it separately in area mode.

Why does the calculator round containers up?

Because paint is sold in fixed cans or pails. Rounding up ensures you have enough to finish the final wall.

What if I prefer not to subtract doors and windows?

Leave both fields at 0. Keeping them at 0 gives you a small buffer for trim and future touch-ups.
