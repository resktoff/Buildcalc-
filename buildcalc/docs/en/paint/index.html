How paint is calculated

First we get the wall area: perimeter × height = 2 × (length + width) × height. If you tick the ceiling, length × width is added.

We subtract openings using typical sizes: 20 ft² (1.9 m²) per door and 16 ft² (1.5 m²) per window. The result is the net area to paint.

The net area is multiplied by the number of coats and divided by the paint coverage (ft² per gallon or m² per litre) stated by the manufacturer.

Finally waste is added and the total is rounded up to whole containers of the chosen size. Rough or very absorbent surfaces may need up to 20% more.

Frequently asked questions

How much paint do I need for a 12 × 10 ft room?

With 8 ft ceilings the walls are 352 ft²; after one door and one window about 316 ft² remain. Two coats at 350 ft²/gal need ≈ 1.8 gal, so two 1-gallon cans.

How many coats of paint should I apply?

Two coats is the standard for an even finish. Over dark colours or new drywall you may need three, or a coat of primer.

What is typical paint coverage?

Most latex and acrylic paints cover 300–400 ft² per gallon per coat (8–12 m² per litre). The exact figure is printed on the can.

Does the calculator work for exteriors?

Yes: use the “Area already known” mode with the façade surface and raise waste to 10–15% for textured stucco or siding.
